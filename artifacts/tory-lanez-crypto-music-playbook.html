What Tory Lanez actually did when he fused crypto with his music — the real mechanics, the unverified headlines, the platform that stranded his fans — and the cleaner, stronger version built for ScrollVerse.

● Sourced / reported ● Artist's claim — unverified ● Documented failure ● Open question

The bottom line: Lanez ran two NFT music experiments in 2021 and one more in early 2022 — then, as far as the public record shows, nothing after that. The replicable mechanics are real and simple: limited-edition, low-price, direct-to-fan album NFTs with card checkout, plus real perks. But the 2021 version is not a template to copy literally. The market that made it work has fallen ~90%+, secondary royalties are no longer reliably enforced, and the exact resale-profit marketing Lanez used is precisely what drew SEC-style scrutiny. The 2026 version is smaller, self-custody-friendly, and marketed as a collectible / access pass — never as an investment.

Two distinct NFT music experiments in 2021, one more in early 2022. What buyers actually got

  • A digital copy of the 7-track album + 7 art pieces, each minted with a one-of-a-kind token as a ledger of ownership
  • Streaming access exclusively inside the E-NFT platform and app — the album was not on Spotify or YouTube at launch
  • A right to resell on E-NFT.com at a price the seller set — framed in the launch release as a “fan opportunity” to “potentially financially benefit from the resale distribution”

What buyers did not get

  • No copyright in the songs
  • No share of Lanez's streaming or publishing royalties
  • No guaranteed return — despite the resale pitch
  • Lanez was to receive a percentage of every resale “indefinitely” — the exact percentage was never stated in any source found. Do not quote one. What is documented is the platform's cut: E-NFT charged sellers a 15% fee on secondary transactions, on top of Ethereum gas.

The drop worth copying: Bondly, March 2021

Most retellings skip this one — and it is arguably the better model for an independent artist. Three unreleased songs, 450 copies, >300 collectors, >$400,000 in initial gross sales and resales. The perks for collecting all three — a virtual meet-and-greet, an unreleased track, signed merchandise — are a fan-club / patronage model, not a speculation model. High revenue per fan, no dependence on mass flipping. That is the element that survives best today.

Conflicting figure, flagged: Celebrity Net Worth says a separate early-2021 Lanez NFT release “reportedly generated $500,000 in initial and secondary-market transactions during its first 24 hours.” That does not match the Bondly-sourced $400,000 figure — this page treats $400,000+ (Bondly / Music Business Worldwide) as the better-sourced number and $500,000 as unverified.

Legal guardrails — read before minting anything

General information from the research, not legal advice. Securities, copyright and tax rules differ by jurisdiction and change frequently — get a lawyer before selling anything that shares revenue or promises returns.

Howey: the single biggest risk

Under SEC v. W.J. Howey Co. (1946), a security exists where there is (i) an investment of money, (ii) in a common enterprise, (iii) with an expectation of profits (iv) from the efforts of others. The SEC applied it to NFTs in 2023:

>$6.1M

Impact Theory — 28 Aug 2023

~$30m raised selling “Founder's Key” NFTs marketed as an investment in the company's growth. Settled for >$6.1m (disgorgement + interest + penalty), no admission.

$1M penalty

Stoner Cats 2 LLC — 13 Sep 2023

10,420 NFTs at ~$800 — ~$8.2m in 35 minutes to fund a web series. $1m civil penalty; tokens destroyed.

$750,000

Flyfish Club — Sept 2024

Penalty in the SEC's NFT enforcement run — same pattern: access NFTs sold with an investment frame.

The 2025–26 shift does not remove the risk: in March 2026 the SEC/CFTC issued a joint interpretive release saying digital collectibles (e.g. CryptoPunks, Chromie Squiggles) are generally not securities — but an NFT sold with a promise of profits, revenue-sharing, yield or fractionalised returns can still be a security. That is agency interpretation, not a statute passed by Congress, and it can change.

Market it like this

  • A limited collectible / access pass that supports the artist
  • Talk about the music, the artwork, the perks
  • Disclose every fee, any lockup, payout timing, and whether the wallet is custodial
  • Disclose any financial interest you hold in the platform
  • Publish only sales numbers you can evidence — keep records of every claim

Never like this

  • No promises of profit, appreciation, “flip” returns, yield, or buy-backs
  • No share of your royalties or revenue — unless run as a registered/exempt securities offering with counsel (Reg A / Reg CF / Reg D). There is no cheap shortcut.
  • Never use “invest”, “ROI”, “passive income” — or “earn a royalty based on how many you hold” (the exact Decentar / 3NITY language)
  • No unverified sell-out or flip-price claims — Lanez's tweets became the evidence Rolling Stone focused on

Copyright & rights

Buying an NFT does not transfer copyright (US Copyright Office / USPTO joint NFT report, Mar 2024). State the buyer's licence in writing in the sale terms; a copyright transfer itself needs a signed written instrument. Clear the underlying rights first — co-writers, producers, sample owners, label/distributor rights in the masters — and split with collaborators by written agreement, not a Discord handshake.

Secondary royalties are not guaranteed

ERC-2981 only signals a royalty — payment is voluntary and marketplace-dependent. OpenSea retired its royalty-enforcement Operator Filter on 31 Aug 2023 and made creator fees optional; Blur and others followed. Enforceable resale income needs a contract-level mechanism (e.g. ERC-721C, or a platform that enforces on its own marketplace like Sound's on-platform royalties) — and even then it only applies where the sale happens.

The ScrollVerse playbook — mirror, match, exceed

Everything Lanez proved, kept. Everything that hurt his buyers, removed.

1 · MIRROR

Copy the mechanics that worked

  • Limited-edition album/EP NFTs, sold direct-to-fan
  • Low, fan-friendly price with card checkout — no wallet needed to buy
  • Bundle: music + unique artwork + exclusive access
  • Primary sale is the revenue event — bypass label/streaming splits on it
2 · MATCH

Match the Bondly energy, not the E-NFT hype

  • Superfan economics: a few hundred collectors, real revenue per fan
  • Collect-the-set perks: meet-and-greet, unreleased track, signed merch
  • Launch-day attention: one drop, one moment, one story
  • Stress-test checkout for launch volume — E-NFT crashed for >24 hours; don't repeat that
3 · EXCEED

Fix every failure he shipped

  • Self-custody from minute one — buyers withdraw to their own wallet immediately; no 28-day lock
  • Music that survives the platform — downloadable files + redundant hosting (IPFS/Arweave + normal download)
  • Credible scarcity — supply matched to real demand, per-buyer cap of 2–3 (Lanez allowed 100)
  • Radical transparency — all fees disclosed up front, low secondary fee, published payout timeline, verifiable sales numbers
  • Streaming window — NFT edition first, streaming after — never strand the music or forfeit chart income

The blueprint, in one paragraph

Mint a small, numbered edition (e.g. 250 copies at $10–$20) of an unreleased EP on your own contract via Sound.xyz / Zora / Manifold on Base; sell by card + crypto, with a per-buyer cap (e.g. 2–3); bundle each copy with the music files (downloadable + streamable), unique artwork, and a real perk (listening party, stems, merch discount, ticket pre-sale); let buyers withdraw to their own wallet immediately; disclose fees and payout timing; market it as a limited collectible that supports the artist — never as an investment or a flip. Put the music on streaming after a short exclusivity window.

Sizing note from the research: for an indie artist the equivalent of Lanez's play is a small edition at a fan-friendly price — e.g. 100–1,000 copies at $5–$25 — not 1,000,000 copies. Lanez could attempt 1m because he had ~18m monthly Spotify listeners; an indie artist's scarcity is the point, and 1m copies would destroy it.

The exceed checklist — Lanez failure → ScrollVerse fix

  • Open, exportable standardERC-721/1155 on an Ethereum L2 (Base, Optimism, Polygon) or Solana — never one startup's closed ledger.
  • Own contractMint via Manifold or equivalent so ScrollVerse keeps control and sets the royalty rules.
  • No lockup, fast payoutsInstant withdrawal; payouts instant or clearly dated (e.g. T+2), published in the sale terms.
  • Fees disclosed, gas negligibleEvery fee in advance; L2 chains make gas a non-issue — no 15% surprise.
  • Verifiable claims onlyOn-chain supply/sales or a third-party sales report behind every number published.
  • Interests disclosedAny stake in the platform is stated publicly; resale value is never promoted.
  • Rights cleared in writingMasters, co-writers, producers and samples cleared before minting; buyer licence written into the terms.
  • Own the relationshipOwn the files, own the mailing list, own the contract — never the only copy of anything inside someone else's app.
